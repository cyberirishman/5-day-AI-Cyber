One House at a Time — How a Neuron Learns from Each Example

The neuron prices a house as price = w · bedrooms + b. Each training step uses one single house: the bedroom count flows forward through the neuron to produce a predicted price, the prediction is compared to the actual price (the error), and the correction flows backward to adjust w and b. Steps alternate between the two houses — this is stochastic gradient descent, exactly how real neural networks train.

* Each step is one round of stochastic gradient descent (batch size 1): forward pass ŷ = w·x + b for one house → error e = ŷ − actual → corrections flow backward: w ← w − η·e·h and the bias by −η·e (learning rate η = 0.3; the input is normalized as h = bedrooms − 3, the average, standard ML practice — w and b are always displayed as the real-dollar values). A neat consequence of two symmetric normalized examples: each house’s error improves only on its own training steps — the two corrections don’t interfere. With many examples the corrections do overlap, which is why full training averages over the whole dataset, as in the companion line-fitting animation.
